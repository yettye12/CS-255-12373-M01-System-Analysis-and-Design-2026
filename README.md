# CS-255-12373-M01-System-Analysis-and-Design-2026

Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?

The DriverPass project was based on a client that wanted to improve the way students prepare for their driving tests. The DriverPass system was designed to provide students with access to driving lessons, practice tests, training materials, and scheduling options. The system needed to allow students to create accounts, purchase packages, schedule driving lessons, select available drivers and vehicles, and manage their appointments. The system also needed to support employees and administrators with managing students, instructors, vehicles, packages, and other information.

What did you do particularly well?

I think I did a good job turning the customer's requirements into diagrams and a system design that showed how the DriverPass system would work. Creating the UML diagrams helped me understand how the different parts of the system would interact with each other. I also think I did well with the activity diagrams because they allowed me to break complicated processes, such as scheduling a driving lesson, into smaller and easier-to-understand steps.

If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

If I could revise one part of my work, I would probably spend more time improving and reviewing my UML diagrams before submitting them. I learned that small details in a diagram can make a big difference in how easy it is for someone else to understand the system. I would make sure the relationships, labels, and flow of each diagram were as clear as possible and that everything matched the requirements in the business requirements document.

How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?

I interpreted the user's needs by looking at what DriverPass wanted the system to accomplish and then breaking those needs down into specific functions. For example, the customer needed an easy way to schedule driving lessons, so the design included the ability to select a package, date, time, driver, and vehicle and then confirm the appointment. I also considered the needs of the employees who would need to manage the system and its information.

Considering the user's needs is important because the system is being created for people to actually use. A system can technically work but still be a bad system if it is difficult or confusing for the users. Understanding the user's needs helps make sure the final system solves the actual problem the customer is trying to solve.

How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

When designing software, I would start by making sure I understand what the customer actually needs before thinking about how to build the system. I would break the requirements into smaller pieces and identify the users, processes, data, and interactions involved. From there, I would use tools such as use case diagrams, activity diagrams, sequence diagrams, and class diagrams to visualize different parts of the system.

In the future, I would also spend more time reviewing the requirements throughout the design process instead of waiting until the end. I think this would help catch misunderstandings earlier and make the final design more consistent. Overall, this project helped me understand that good software design starts with understanding the problem and the people who will be using the system.
